How we run a shoot

A calm shoot day. Nothing rushed, nothing wasted.

A clear brief keeps a shoot personal rather than generic. We settle the purpose of the pictures first, then build the light, the set, and the schedule around them.

01 /

Start with the brief

We ask what the images are for, where they will be used, who is in them, and what needs to be true for the shoot to be a success.

02 /

Plan the light and the set

References, wardrobe, props, location, and a realistic shot list — all agreed before the day, so nobody is guessing on set.

03 /

Shoot with direction, not orders

We guide continuously and keep the room easy. The best frames usually happen two seconds after the pose you were asked to hold.

04 /

Edit and deliver

A curated selection, consistent retouching, and files supplied in the formats and sizes you actually need — print, web, or both.

A few useful answers

Before you book a shoot.

A good session depends on the right brief, not a fixed package. Start with the practical questions.

Ask a shoot-specific question ↗
I am really not comfortable in front of a camera.

That is the normal starting point. We direct throughout the session — where to look, what to do with your hands, when to move — and we build in enough time that you can settle. Nobody is expected to arrive ready.

How long does a portrait session take?

A single headshot sitting usually takes about an hour including setup and review. A fuller personal brand session, with outfit changes and multiple setups, is typically two to three hours.

Can you shoot at our office, venue, or home?

Yes. We work both in the studio and on location. For location shoots we ask for a short recce, or a set of phone photos of the space, so the lighting plan is realistic before the day.

How many edited images do we receive?

It depends on the scope of the shoot and is agreed in the proposal before booking. You receive a curated selection rather than every frame captured, retouched consistently as a set.

Do you photograph products and interiors as well?

We do. Product, still life, architecture, and interior work all sit within the studio — often with the same lighting approach used for portraits, just with a different subject.

Begin with what matters

Make pictures that actually look like you.

Tell us what the images are for, when you need them, and roughly what you have in mind. We can start by defining a sensible scope for your shoot and your budget.
